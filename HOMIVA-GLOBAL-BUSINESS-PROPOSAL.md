# HOMIVA — Global Business & Product Proposal

**Prepared:** 7 October 2026  
**Working product name:** HOMIVA  
**Positioning:** *Find where life fits.*  
**Status:** Founder proposal; no public launch, inventory, revenue, partnerships, or legal clearance is claimed by this document.

## 1. Executive proposal

HOMIVA is a housing decision platform that helps people find a home, understand its real monthly cost, see what is known or unverified, compare options, contact the owner, and arrange a viewing. Owners get a simple way to create and manage their own listings and enquiries.

The opportunity is to compete on **decision quality and trust**, not on a claim of being the biggest portal on day one. Existing businesses demonstrate several strong models: NoBroker emphasizes direct owner-to-renter discovery in India; Zillow combines discovery with rental-management workflows in the US; Rightmove serves a mature UK property-search market; Airbnb demonstrates paid transaction services for stays. HOMIVA should learn from these categories while keeping its own brand and focusing on transparent cost, useful evidence, and a clear path from search to viewing. [NoBroker](https://www.nobroker.in/about/about-us/), [Zillow Rentals](https://www.zillow.com/rentals-network/), [Rightmove](https://www.rightmove.co.uk/press-centre/about-rightmove/), [Airbnb fees](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1857)

**Recommendation:** launch a focused, owner-supplied rental marketplace in one city; prove listing freshness and successful renter-owner connections; then add professional tools and expand city by city. Keep the technology capable of international markets, but never imply HOMIVA has listings, verification, legal coverage, or operations where it does not.

## 2. The customer problem

Renters often compare incomplete listings, unclear recurring costs, stale availability, uncertain owner claims, and fragmented ways to contact a landlord. Owners must repeatedly write listings, answer the same questions, and coordinate visits. A bigger list alone does not solve the decision.

HOMIVA turns the journey into:

**Tell us what matters → find matching owner-posted homes → understand cost and unknowns → compare → contact → request a viewing.**

The product must label listing facts by source and state. “Owner provided” is different from “HOMIVA checked”; “estimated” is different from “known.” Payment must never buy a trust badge.

## 3. Product and defensible position

### Renter experience

- Search in ordinary language and refine with structured filters.
- Browse in list, map, or split view.
- Compare rent, deposit, maintenance, furnishing, area, and known/unknown costs.
- Save homes, message the owner, and request a viewing.
- See approximate public map pins and honest verification status.

### Owner experience

- Create an account and a listing, add a location and photos, preview, publish, and edit.
- Receive messages and viewing requests in one dashboard.
- See a future path to freshness reminders, portfolio tools, lead analytics, and team access.

### Product advantage to earn

HOMIVA can build an evidence-backed **living graph**: relationships between homes, locations, affordability, amenities, commute context, owner updates, and completed housing steps. Build it with consented, first-party information and clear provenance. Do not scrape competitor inventories or expose sensitive household information.

## 4. Market and launch strategy

The UN’s 2025 urbanization revision estimates that cities were home to 45% of the world’s 8.2 billion people in 2025, and projects 48% of a 9.7 billion world population in cities by 2050. This is context for a long-term housing platform, not a HOMIVA addressable-market or revenue calculation. [UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025](https://population.un.org/wup/?stream=future)

### Staged entry

1. **Choose one Indian launch city.** Compare owner supply, renter demand, founder access, local competition, operating cost, and applicable rental rules. The city is intentionally left open until the founder validates supply; the current website uses INR and must not be presented as globally localized.
2. **Recruit supply before broad demand marketing.** Interview owners and renters, confirm listing rights, publish genuine homes with owners’ permission, and establish a human support and report-review process.
3. **Operate a limited public beta.** Measure stale listings, first owner response, qualified enquiries, completed viewings, and support incidents. Keep trust labels conservative.
4. **Expand regionally only after the first city has repeatable listing acquisition, freshness checks, renter outcomes, and support coverage.** Add country-specific legal, currency, language, payments, and data handling before entering another jurisdiction.

Do not advertise global inventory just because the website is reachable worldwide. Internet reach is global; service availability and listings remain market-specific.

## 5. Competitive strategy

| Category | What customers already get | HOMIVA’s focused response |
|---|---|---|
| Large property portals | Broad inventory, filters, area discovery, agent and owner listings | Make cost completeness, freshness, source, and unknowns visible at the decision point |
| Direct-owner marketplaces | Owner-to-renter contact and reduced intermediary friction | Keep owner identity/role claims explicit; provide a useful message and viewing workflow |
| Short-stay marketplaces | Reservation, service, and payment flows | Start with rental discovery; add transaction or stay services only after local operations and compliance exist |
| Property-management software | Portfolio workflows, tenant screening, leases, rent collection | Start with renter-owner connection; add professional SaaS only when owner interviews reveal repeatable paid value |

This is a positioning hypothesis, not proof that HOMIVA is better than every incumbent. Validate it through interviews, observed conversion, and repeat usage. Do not use competitor names or marks as HOMIVA branding.

## 6. Business model

Keep core renter discovery free while inventory and outcomes are being proven. Do not collect rent, deposits, or card details in the current product.

| Stage | Revenue option | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot | Free basic listing and search | Remove avoidable signup friction; no fake paid tier |
| Repeat owner value | Configurable monthly professional plan for multi-property tools, workflow, and analytics | Charge only after measurable owner value; publish plan and cancellation terms |
| Mature marketplace | Clearly labeled promoted placement or qualified lead tools | Paid visibility must not masquerade as trust or organic ranking |
| Later platform | Property-management SaaS, integrations, or carefully selected service referrals | Enter only with local compliance, consent, support, and partner disclosure |

Do not launch mandatory hidden fees, unreviewed paid “verification,” intrusive ad targeting, or a rental transaction fee before the product controls a valuable, compliant transaction.

### Unit economics to measure (do not invent)

- **Owner acquisition cost** = attributable supply-acquisition spend ÷ activated owners.
- **Renter acquisition cost** = attributable renter-acquisition spend ÷ qualified renters.
- **Qualified enquiry rate** = property-specific enquiries with a valid account ÷ listing detail views.
- **Viewing conversion** = confirmed viewings ÷ qualified enquiries.
- **Revenue per professional owner** = collected subscription revenue ÷ paying professional owners.
- **Contribution margin** = revenue less payment, support, verification, storage, and acquisition costs.
- **Payback / LTV / churn** should be reported only after enough real cohorts exist.

## 7. North-star metric and operating dashboard

**North star:** verified, user-confirmed successful housing connections per active market per month. A successful connection should be defined before launch (for example, both sides confirm a viewing or rental outcome); a click or message alone is not success.

Weekly dashboard: published fresh listings; listing age and availability confirmation; search-to-detail rate; detail-to-qualified-enquiry rate; owner first-response time; enquiry-to-viewing conversion; renter and owner repeat use; report and suspected-fraud rate; time to resolve reports; and platform availability. Financial dashboard after monetization: MRR, gross margin, owner CAC, renter CAC, churn, LTV, and payback.

Use privacy-preserving aggregate product analytics only after deciding the purpose, retention, consent, and applicable legal basis. The current product has no analytics vendor configured.

## 8. Product generations and roadmap

| Horizon | Outcome | Build only when |
|---|---|---|
| **Now — Rental marketplace** | Search, filters, map, cost breakdown, listing, accounts, saved homes, messaging, viewing requests | Pilot city and owner-provided inventory are ready |
| **Next — Decision intelligence** | Saved searches, freshness alerts, comparable homes, explainable match reasons, localization resources | There is enough current, comparable listing data and clear user consent |
| **Trust operations** | Owner-role checks, property evidence review, duplicate/report queues, audit trail, moderation service levels | Human reviewers, evidence rules, appeal process, and local policy exist |
| **Living workflow** | Rental passport, document summaries, lease steps, maintenance requests | Legal review, data minimization, document security, and opt-in are in place |
| **Platform** | Professional SaaS, integrations, partner services, APIs | Repeatable economics, service quality, and country-level controls are proven |
| **Global living layer** | Multiple countries, currencies, languages, locality/commute intelligence | Each country has real supply, trusted data sources, local support, and compliance coverage |

AI should extract or summarize information with the source visible, state uncertainty, and ask for confirmation before acting. It must not invent availability, verification, legal advice, rent affordability, or an opaque “match score.”

## 9. Trust, safety, privacy, and compliance

- Keep owner-supplied information clearly separate from HOMIVA-reviewed evidence.
- Build report, review, escalation, and appeal operations before inviting unrestricted public inventory.
- Keep exact property coordinates private where appropriate; expose only the precision needed for discovery.
- Collect only necessary account, listing, enquiry, and viewing data; publish retention and deletion rules.
- Before serving people in the EU, assess GDPR scope and obligations; EU guidance says it can apply to organisations outside the EU that offer services to or monitor people in the EU. [Your Europe — GDPR for businesses](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/governance-and-sustainability/digital-and-data-compliance/data-protection-gdpr/index_en.htm)
- Use WCAG 2.2 as an accessibility design and QA baseline. [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/)
- Track security work against OWASP ASVS; a clean dependency scan does not establish security certification. [OWASP ASVS](https://owasp.org/projects/asvs)
- Have qualified counsel review the operator entity, terms, privacy notice, listing rules, fair-housing obligations, consumer disclosures, and country-specific property rules. This proposal is not legal advice.

## 10. First 90 days: founder execution plan

### Days 1–30: validate the market

- Select a launch city from founder access and documented supply/demand interviews.
- Speak to prospective owners, renters, and local operators; record top unmet needs and willingness to pay.
- Secure permission for a small, genuine listing cohort and a human contact for every listing.
- Finalize legal operator identity, public support address, domain, policies, and an incident/report owner.

### Days 31–60: run a controlled beta

- Invite a limited renter cohort; personally monitor listing freshness, replies, and reports.
- Watch actual search-to-enquiry and enquiry-to-viewing journeys.
- Remove duplicate, stale, misleading, or unauthorized inventory promptly.
- Improve the top friction points from user observation, not feature count.

### Days 61–90: decide whether to repeat

- Review outcomes, report rates, owner retention, and cost to obtain/maintain a listing.
- Test pricing conversations with professionals; do not bill until value and terms are clear.
- Expand the first city only if freshness, support, and liquidity remain healthy; otherwise fix the supply or workflow problem.
- Produce a real pilot scorecard before making investor or public traction claims.

## 11. Funding and use of funds

No funding amount, valuation, current revenue, traction, or forecast is stated because none was supplied or verified. A financing proposal should be completed once the founder supplies runway, target raise, team plan, and launch-market budget. Initial spending should be staged against milestones for product/security, supply operations, customer support, legal review, and market validation. Keep a cash runway model with downside cases before accepting external money.

## 12. Decisions required before public launch

- Legal operator name, incorporation jurisdiction, registered/contact address, and support/privacy contact.
- First operating country and city, rental-only scope, language(s), and local currency.
- Hosting provider/account, domain/DNS access, billing owner, and a named person responsible for renewals.
- Initial owner supply and permission evidence; human listing/report review coverage.
- Final reviewed privacy notice, terms, community/listing rules, and local fair-housing policy.
- Backup/restore owner, incident contact, access-control owner, and published support response expectations.
- Domain, company-name, trademark, and social-handle clearance; no availability is claimed here.

## 13. Long-term vision

**A good place. At a fair cost. From a trustworthy person. With enough information, less risk, and a process that fits real life.**

HOMIVA’s goal can be to become the most trusted housing-decision platform in the markets it serves. “Number one” is an ambition to earn market by market through real housing outcomes—not a guarantee or a launch-day claim.
